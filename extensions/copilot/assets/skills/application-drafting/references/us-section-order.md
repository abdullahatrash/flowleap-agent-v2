# US section order (37 CFR 1.77(b))

Write the Draft Application in this order. 37 CFR 1.77(b) also lists the
transmittal forms, the application data sheet and the fee forms: those are
not text of the draft. A section with no content for this invention is left
out, except the ones marked required.

| # | Heading | Content and source |
|---|---------|--------------------|
| 1 | Title (required) | Short and technical, from the Feature List. |
| 2 | CROSS-REFERENCE TO RELATED APPLICATIONS | Only when the attorney gives the priority or related applications (`instruction`). Never infer one. |
| 3 | STATEMENT REGARDING FEDERALLY SPONSORED RESEARCH OR DEVELOPMENT | Only on attorney instruction. |
| 4 | THE NAMES OF THE PARTIES TO A JOINT RESEARCH AGREEMENT | Only on attorney instruction. |
| 5 | Reference to a sequence listing, table or computer program listing appendix | Only when the disclosure has one. |
| 6 | STATEMENT REGARDING PRIOR DISCLOSURES BY THE INVENTOR OR A JOINT INVENTOR | Only on attorney instruction (bar-date work of the invention-disclosure skill). |
| 7 | BACKGROUND (required) | Technical field, then the known art the disclosure names. Describe the art; make no admission that something is prior art beyond what the disclosure states. |
| 8 | SUMMARY (required) | The invention as claimed, one paragraph per independent claim, in the claims' own terms. |
| 9 | BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS (required when figures exist) | One sentence per figure from `figures.md`: "FIG. 1 is a ... view of ...". |
| 10 | DETAILED DESCRIPTION (required) | Every claim element with its embodiments, alternatives and examples from the Feature List, using the numerals of `figures.md`. Working examples in past tense only when the inventor ran them; prophetic examples in present or future tense. |
| 11 | CLAIMS (required) | The Approved Claims, unchanged. Heading contains "Claims". Starts on a new page in the export. |
| 12 | ABSTRACT (required) | At most 150 words (37 CFR 1.72(b)), one paragraph, no claim language such as "said" or "means". Heading contains "Abstract". Starts on a new page in the export. |

## US practice notes

- **No "Objects of the Invention" section.** The US template states no
  objects or purposes of the invention: a stated object can be read as a
  claim limitation. The problem goes in the Background as known art's
  shortcoming, in the disclosure's terms.
- Section headings are in capitals, without bold or underline (37 CFR
  1.77(c)).
- Avoid "the invention is", "must", "essential", "critical" about a feature
  unless the disclosure says so: such words narrow the claims.
- Every claim term appears in the Detailed Description in the same words
  (literal basis); the validators check this.
