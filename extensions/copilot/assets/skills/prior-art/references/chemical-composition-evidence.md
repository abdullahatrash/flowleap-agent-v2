# Chemical Composition and Formulation Evidence

Worked rulings for Phase 3 relevance assessment when the invention is a composition, formulation
or a process defined by composition. They are applications of the general evidence principles in
`SKILL.md`, not extra rules: preserve the source's qualifiers, units and denominators, keep a
dependent-claim limitation inside its own claim, and bound absence to the passages actually read.

## Narrower term versus the claim's term

A conclusion that turns on a narrower species needs a passage reciting that species. A claim that
recites only the genus does not supply it.

- The conclusion says **photoinitiator**; claim 1 recites only "polymerization initiator".
  The claim alone is insufficient support. Attach the description or example that names the
  photoinitiator, and keep the claim's broader scope in the recorded evidence — do not restate
  claim 1 as if it recited the species.
- The same holds in reverse: an example naming one species does not narrow the claim that recites
  the genus, and must not be reported as a claim limitation.
- The conclusion needs a **fine-fraction limit** (for example "at least 30 wt% of the filler below
  2 µm"); the passage recites only "filler", with no size classification. An unclassified filler
  does not disclose a classified one. Cite the passage stating the size distribution, or leave the
  row partial and name the missing classification in the gap.
- The same shape covers a composition element: a recited "filling material" does not disclose a
  dental filling material, and a recited "initiator" does not disclose a light-curing initiator.

## A dependent claim's number stays in that dependent claim

- "Claim 8: the composition of claim 1, wherein the filler is 20-40 wt%." That range belongs to
  the claim 1 + claim 8 combination. It must not be attributed to claim 1, and it does not make
  20-40 wt% a requirement of every composition the publication discloses.
- Judge a dependent claim's scope only after reading the parent claims it cites. A preferred
  embodiment or example carries the same limit: preferred is not mandatory.

## Denominators, quantity basis and conversions

- Keep parts as parts. "100 parts monomer, 2 parts initiator, 50 parts filler" is not a
  weight-percent of the whole composition, and converting it requires accounting for every
  constituent, including catalysts, initiators, stabilizers and other additives.
- Record which basis the source used, per source. Two publications reporting "30%" may use
  different denominators; a comparison that silently merges them is wrong.
- A stated average does not establish a distribution or a mass fraction. A median particle size
  says nothing about how much material sits below a threshold.

## Exclusion and qualifying language

- "Essentially free of X" is not "contains no X" and is not "complete exclusion". Quote the
  qualifier as written and assess the feature against that wording.
- "Substantially", "about", "preferably" and open transitions such as "comprising" change scope.
  Never drop them when recording evidence.

## Range overlap

An overlap between a claimed range and the invention's range is **partial** support, not a match.
Record the two ranges and the extent of the overlap; leave the row partial unless a passage
establishes the specific value the conclusion needs.

## Absence statements about a process step

A missing literal match establishes only that the term was not found in the text you searched.
Before recording "no classification step" or "no sieving step", read the later description
passages that describe post-processing — process steps are often described without the term the
query used. Bound the statement to the passages actually reviewed.
