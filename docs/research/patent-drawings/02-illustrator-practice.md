# Patent illustrator practice: the contract the feature must match

Date: 2026-10-12. Scope: how a small-firm attorney works with an outsourced patent illustrator today, for utility drawings in the US and Europe.

Source tiers used below. **[A]** patent-office rule or guidance. **[B]** law-firm or practitioner page. **[C]** illustrator firm's own order, FAQ or price page. **[D]** marketplace listing, vendor blog or vendor "guide" (marketing; numbers unverified). Every bullet carries a URL.

## 1. What the attorney sends the illustrator

- **Accepted inputs (illustrator first-party).** The Patent Drawing Services FAQ: "We accept all inputs be it rough/crude/handmade sketches, photographs, videos, writeup of the invention, screenshots etc." [C] https://thepatentdrawing.com/faq/
- **Accepted inputs (German firm).** Maas Patente takes "einfache Skizze, Powerpoint oder Word" (simple sketch, PowerPoint or Word), plus "a reference numeral list if you have one"; it may ask for DXF files, details or revisions. [C] https://maas-patent.de/patentzeichnung/
- **Accepted inputs (CAD firm).** DesignPresentation lists "blue prints, hand drawn sketches, rough sketches, photographs, prototypes, engineering drawings, verbal descriptions, CAD files"; accepts IGS and converts output to DWG or DXF. [C] https://designpresentation.com/?p=100
- **CAD formats named.** A vendor guide calls native SOLIDWORKS or AutoCAD files "ideal" for mechanical inventions; for software inventions it asks for block diagrams, flowcharts, architecture diagrams and UI wireframes. [D] https://www.perspireip.com/blog/how-to-work-with-patent-illustrator/
- **CAD-only tools.** Pinch is "optimized for CAD-based inputs only"; hand sketches and photos "require a traditional illustrator". [D] https://pinchpatentdrawings.com/top-patent-drawing-services-providers/
- **Prior patents as style reference.** Attorney J.D. Houvener: send "photos, sketches, even rough shots of a prototype" and "if there's a similar product or an issued patent you know about, send that too." [B] https://boldip.com/blog/patent-drawings-secrets-every-inventor-needs/
- **Numeral key and claim map.** The vendor guide tells the attorney to "Prepare a reference numeral key ... and instruct the illustrator to apply those numerals consistently across all figures", send the draft claims, a claim-element-to-feature map, and a list of required views. [D] https://www.perspireip.com/blog/how-to-work-with-patent-illustrator/
- **Clarification pattern.** LegalZoom's intake asks "What you want the drawings to convey", "Any specific elements you want included", "Any sketches or photographs". [C] https://www.legalzoom.com/business/intellectual-property/professional-patent-drawings-overview.html A freelancer listing: "If additional details are needed, I'll let you know before starting." [D] https://fiverr.com/marcels2/create-uspto-utility-patent-drawings-with-precise-patent-figures The Patent Drawing Services lets the client "directly speak to the draftsman". [C] https://thepatentdrawing.com/faq/
- **No first-party "what we ask when unclear" page was found.** The observed pattern is: confirm figure count, target office and views before work starts, then ask for function and moving-part detail for exploded and sectional views. [D] https://www.upwork.com/services/product/design-uspto-epo-patent-drawings-technical-line-art-vector-illustration-1998535463562096219

## 2. What comes back

- **Filing format is PDF.** USPTO Patent Center accepts PDF, "a minimum resolution of 300 DPI", A4 or 8.5 x 11, layers flattened, fonts embedded; "3-dimensional models (e.g., CAD drawings)" are prohibited. [A] https://www.uspto.gov/patents/apply/applying-online/efs-web-pdf-guidelines
- **Office image formats differ.** IP5 table: USPTO = PDF (GIF/BMP/TIFF/PNG only inside the PDF), EPO = PDF per PCT Annex F, PCT = TIFF or JFIF at 300 or 400 dpi; IP5-safe resolution 300 dpi. [A] https://link.epo.org/ip5/IP5_Allowable%20Features%20in%20Drawings_20230331_revised.pdf
- **Illustrator deliverables.** "PTO accepts PDF format, but AI, DWG, PPT, VSD, JPEG, CAD, and CDR formats can also be delivered at no cost." [C] https://thepatentdrawing.com/faq/ Effectual Services lists PDF, TIFF, JPEG and Word. [D] https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/437936/how-to-identify-a-good-patent-illustrator Patent Drafting Catalyst: "editable formats may also be available on request". [C] https://patentdraftingcatalyst.com/patent-drawing-services/ Pinch outputs DXF, "compatible with Visio, CorelDRAW, and Adobe Illustrator". [D] https://pinchpatentdrawings.com/top-patent-drawing-services-providers/
- **Attorney-side demand for editable source.** McBee Moore & Vanik: obtain "original high-resolution images and editable source files for flowcharts and drawings"; "'Snipping' images out of PDFs ... will often result in very low-resolution images." [B] https://mmviplaw.com/2019/06/13/tips-for-preparing-compliant-drawings-in-u-s-patent-applications/
- **Sheet format.** US Letter or A4, one side, margins top 2.5 cm, left 2.5 cm, right 1.5 cm, bottom 1.0 cm, no frame around the sight, sheet number "1/3" style, views numbered "FIG. 1" consecutively. [A] https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/1.84 EPO/PCT: A4, usable area max 26.2 x 17.0 cm, same margins. [A] https://www.wipo.int/pct/en/texts/rules/r11.html DesignPresentation delivers "A4 sheet (8.27 x 11.69) or letter (8.5 x 11)". [C] https://designpresentation.com/?p=100
- **Colour and greyscale at the EPO since 1 October 2025**, if "clearly displayed at a resolution of 300 dpi"; paper filings stay black and white. [A] https://www.epo.org/en/legal/official-journal/2025/07/a49
- **Revisions.** "The iteration is FREE, and you will receive the revised drawing within 1-2 working days"; office-action changes also free. [C] https://thepatentdrawing.com/faq/ Maas: corrections "sofort" (immediate). [C] https://maas-patent.de/patentzeichnung/ A Canadian guide prices post-objection revisions at CAD 50-100. [D] https://lawyerinfo.ca/guides/money-taxes-ip/intellectual-property/the-role-of-a-patent-draftsman-in-canada/ Vendor guide: "one to three rounds of revisions", feedback "specific and numbered". [D] https://www.perspireip.com/blog/how-to-work-with-patent-illustrator/ Pinch: no revision service, edit the DXF yourself. [D] https://pinchpatentdrawings.com/top-patent-drawing-services-providers/

## 4. Tools and 2D versus 3D

- **Named tool set.** AutoCAD, CorelDRAW, Visio, SolidWorks (Effectual Services). [D] https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/437936/how-to-identify-a-good-patent-illustrator TinkerCAD, CREO, CorelDraw, SolidWorks, AutoCAD, CATIA, DraftSight (IAM Insight, same firm). [D] https://www.iam-media.com/article/why-software-illustration-tools-can-be-key-securing-patent-grants Arrow Patent Design: "SolidWorks, Visio, Illustrator, and 3D scanning". [D] https://pinchpatentdrawings.com/top-patent-drawing-services-providers/
- **Why CAD.** AutoCAD imports "STP, IGS, Parasolid, and STEP" and "can swiftly transform 3D files into 2D line drawings"; block libraries reuse recurring parts. [D] https://sagaciousresearch.com/blog/best-patent-drawing-software-for-error-free-illustrations
- **Why a vector finisher.** The 3D-to-2D handoff in practice: place the assembly "into a 2D drawing placed in a particular position", export DXF or PDF, finish in "Corel or Illustrator"; CAD exports are "a big mess of curves" for outlining. [D, user forum] https://community.coreldraw.com/talk/coreldraw_graphics_suite_x8/f/coreldraw-x8/56449/technical-drawing-style---line-art-with-two-line-weight/270982
- **Why 2D by hand still exists.** Line shading has no one-click CAD feature; Illustrator art and pattern brushes are used. [D, user forum] https://community.adobe.com/t5/illustrator/quickest-way-to-create-line-shading-for-patent-drawings/m-p/9956209 Pinch "cannot yet produce line shading" and no exploded or sectional views. [D] https://pinchpatentdrawings.com/top-patent-drawing-services-providers/
- **Model-first freelancers.** "If you have a rough sketch or concept, I can turn it into a realistic 3D model, which I then use to create professional drawings." [D] https://www.fiverr.com/robert_jame12
- **Flowchart tools.** "Dedicated drawing and flow chart tools, such as Microsoft Visio, are much better"; build on a page already at final size and margins. [B] https://mmviplaw.com/2019/06/13/tips-for-preparing-compliant-drawings-in-u-s-patent-applications/ draw.io, Lucidchart and PowerPoint produce acceptable figures if exported as solid black lines. [D] https://madepatents.com/guides/patent-drawings/
- **Line weights the trade targets.** IP5-safe: solid lines approx 0.4 mm, leader and dashed lines approx 0.2 mm; all details must survive reduction to two-thirds. [A] https://link.epo.org/ip5/IP5_Allowable%20Features%20in%20Drawings_20230331_revised.pdf

## 5. The illustrator's pre-delivery QA

No firm publishes its own checklist. The items below are the rule points the USPTO trains examiners on and the office form checks, which is what a careful illustrator checks against.

- **USPTO examiner training (TC 2900, FY2023)** walks 37 CFR 1.84(a)-(b) line drawing vs photograph vs CAD rendering ("CAD renderings should be first assessed by the photorealistic quality"), (h)(1) exploded views in brackets, (h)(2) enlarged views labelled separately, (h)(3) section plane marked with a broken line and arrows, hatching "regularly spaced oblique parallel lines", (k) legible at two-thirds reduction and no "Scale 1/2" labels, (l) lines "clean, black ... uniformly thick", (m) shading thin and sparse, no solid black, (p) numerals plain and legible, (u) views numbered consecutively "FIG. 1", a single view unnumbered. No frequency statistics are in the deck. [A] https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/tc2900_drawing_objections_fy23.pdf
- **Rule text to check.** Margins and no frame (g); as many views as necessary (h); views not overlapping (i); symbols for conventional elements (n); legends "as few words as possible" (o); characters at least 0.32 cm (p)(3); same part, same character in every view (p)(4); no character absent from the description (p)(5); lead lines start near the character and "must not cross each other" (q); sheets numbered within the sight (t). [A] https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/1.84
- **EPO / PCT.** Lines "durable, black, sufficiently dense and dark, uniformly thick and well-defined"; numerals at least 0.32 cm; no brackets, circles or inverted commas around signs; figures numbered consecutively independent of sheets; a sign absent from the description "must not appear" in the drawings; a separate reference-sign list is "strongly recommended" for sign-heavy drawings. [A] https://www.wipo.int/pct/en/texts/rules/r11.html EPO Guidelines A-IX 7.5: signs "not used in association with any brackets, inverted commas, circles or outlines whatsoever"; primes such as 6' are allowed. [A] https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2024/a_ix_7_5.html Usable area 26.2 x 17 cm. [A] https://www.epo.org/law-practice/legal-texts/html/epc/2016/e/r46.html
- **UK IPO factsheet.** Black well-defined lines, capitals at least 0.3 cm, margins 2.0 / 2.0 / 1.5 / 1.0 cm, sheets "1/3", "same reference number ... in all figures", no descriptive text, flow charts acceptable, engineering drawings with dimensions "not normally suitable". [A] https://www.gov.uk/guidance/patent-factsheet-drawings
- **Vendor checklist shape (about 24 items).** Paper size per office, margins, sheet numbers, single-sided, black lines at 300-600 DPI, hatching angles, no colour, every numeral matched to the spec, leads not crossing, every embodiment and claim element drawn, brief description matched to figures, logos removed. [D] https://www.maxval.com/patent-drawings-complete-guide-to-compliance/ Print one sheet at actual size and look. [D] https://dev.to/local_ai_28441e061d716cb1/7-common-patent-drawing-defects-that-trigger-objections-and-how-to-fix-each-one-4h8c

## 6. The attorney-side review and what goes wrong

- **Statutory gates.** "The drawing in a nonprovisional application must show every feature of the invention specified in the claims" (37 CFR 1.83(a), MPEP 608.02(d)). The examiner "should ensure that the figures are correctly described in the brief description of the several views", that "no single reference character is used for two different parts", and that "there are no superfluous illustrations" (MPEP 608.02(e)). [A] https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s608.html
- **The five standard objections** (MPEP 608.02(e) form paragraphs): 6.22.01 details described but not shown; 6.22.02 two numbers for one part; 6.22.03 one number for two parts; 6.22.06 numbers in the description missing from the drawings; 6.22.07 numbers in the drawings missing from the description. [B] https://blueironip.com/ufaqs/what-are-common-objections-to-patent-drawings/
- **Review steps practitioners state.** Check technical compliance under 1.84; read the detailed description and confirm each numeral is depicted; "confirm every element of each independent claim appears in at least one figure"; alternative embodiments drawn; a skilled person could understand the invention from the drawings alone. [D] https://www.perspireip.com/blog/how-to-work-with-patent-illustrator/ "Our patent attorney reviews the drawing plan alongside the proposed disclosure and claims"; "Match reference numerals and figure descriptions to the specification." [B] https://carsonpatents.com/patents/patent-drawings/ Keep a master numeral list; check text-to-figures and figures-to-text. [D] https://madepatents.com/guides/patent-drawings/
- **What typically goes wrong (law firm).** Brief description cites "FIG. 1" while sheets carry FIG. 1A and 1B, or the reverse; margins or page size below minimum; text under 1/8 inch; overlapping or mixed-orientation views; reference numbers larger than "FIG." labels; unapproved colour; low-resolution snips. [B] https://mmviplaw.com/2019/06/13/tips-for-preparing-compliant-drawings-in-u-s-patent-applications/
- **What typically goes wrong (European firm).** Text in flowchart boxes draws formal objections and raises translation cost; poor contrast, numerals in circles, missing Arabic figure numbers; adding figures after filing "may entail the loss of this original filing date"; hand-drawn figures with amendments. Clients "typically supply the figures" and the firm adapts them. [B] https://abg-ip.com/figures-for-patents/
- **Statistics.** A vendor claims "37% of first USPTO office actions include at least one drawing objection" and "Margin violations are the #1 drawing rejection reason", both unsourced. [D] https://www.maxval.com/patent-drawings-complete-guide-to-compliance/ The USPTO training deck gives no frequencies. [A] https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/tc2900_drawing_objections_fy23.pdf No official objection statistics were found.
- **Timing rule.** "If time is short, file available drawings and revise later"; compliant drawings up front are cheaper. [B] https://mmviplaw.com/2019/06/13/tips-for-preparing-compliant-drawings-in-u-s-patent-applications/

## 7. Flowcharts and block diagrams for software and method claims

- **They are drawings.** PCT Rule 11.11: drawings may carry only words such as "water", "steam", "open", and "in the case of electric circuits and block schematic or flow sheet diagrams, a few short catchwords indispensable for understanding"; words must be placeable so they "could be pasted over without touching the drawing lines". [A] https://www.wipo.int/pct/en/texts/rules/r11.html USPTO: symbols for conventional elements and legends with "as few words as possible". [A] https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/1.84 UK IPO cites "a flow chart of method steps" as an example drawing. [A] https://www.gov.uk/guidance/patent-factsheet-drawings
- **Who draws them.** The attorney or inventor usually draws them in Visio or similar and sends the editable file; dedicated flow-chart tools beat presentation software. [B] https://mmviplaw.com/2019/06/13/tips-for-preparing-compliant-drawings-in-u-s-patent-applications/ Illustrators then add numerals and leader lines, which general flowchart tools do not handle. [D] https://dev.to/local_ai_28441e061d716cb1/what-figures-does-a-software-patent-need-a-claim-to-diagram-map-with-prompts-3poc
- **What they look like.** One box per claimed step, numerals matching the specification; "flowchart labels should agree with the application's terminology". [B] https://carsonpatents.com/patents/patent-drawings/ Block diagrams show "the general parts of a system and how they relate", well-known parts without detail; method patents "often have flowcharts which give the steps of the method". [B] https://bpmlegal.com/howtopat3.html Uniform box widths and equal arrow lengths; a START and END marker is the drafting-service default "unless otherwise instructed by the client", and not every chart needs them. [D] https://ipwatchdog.com/2020/12/01/understand-utility-patent-application-drawings/
- **Text in boxes.** US practice tolerates short step labels; European counsel advises "Minimise the text in the figures" and put "all the text that must be in the figure ... in the description", for both formal and translation reasons. [B] https://abg-ip.com/figures-for-patents/
- **Step numbering.** Steps take reference numerals in the figure's hundred series (102, 104, 106 in FIG. 1); a flowchart-numbering patent states the convention that "the most significant digit or digits in a reference number refer to the figure number in which that element is first introduced". [A, patent text] https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10360200

## 8. How numerals are assigned

- **No rule fixes the scheme.** 37 CFR 1.84(p) only requires legible characters at least 0.32 cm, one character per part across views, and no character absent from the description. [A] https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/1.84 Attorney answers: "there is no rule on such numbering, the goal is ease of reference." [B] https://www.avvo.com/legal-answers/when-drafting-a-patent-diagram-are-random-numbers--2212767.html
- **Even numbers only, why.** Practitioner: "I use even numbers only so that if I decide to reference something else later, I can use an odd number appropriately positioned within the sequence." [B] https://www.avvo.com/legal-answers/when-drafting-a-patent-diagram-are-random-numbers--2212767.html Vendor drafting guide: use even or odd numerals in sequence (2, 4, 6, 8) to leave room for later additions. [D] https://www.copperpodip.com/post/patent-drafting-do-s-and-don-ts Automated drafting tools encode the same habit: each new element gets "the next even number". [A, patent text] https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7890851
- **Figure-prefixed hundreds, why.** "most attorneys number all the elements in Fig. 1 as 100, 110, 112, 120, etc., and Fig 2 as 200, 202, 204". [B] https://www.avvo.com/legal-answers/when-drafting-a-patent-diagram-are-random-numbers--2212767.html The leading digits name the figure of first appearance, so a reader finds the element without a lookup. [A, patent text] https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10360200 A hierarchical variant uses tens for assemblies and units for sub-parts (10, then 11, 12). [D] https://madepatents.com/guides/patent-drawings/
- **Variants and suffixes.** Repeated instances take letter suffixes (71a, 71b); primes (71', 71") are the older style; an underlined numeral marks the surface it sits on; a lead line to nothing in particular means the whole device. [B] https://bpmlegal.com/howtopat3.html PCT: "6'" and "35\"" are permitted, brackets and circles are not. [A] https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2024/a_ix_7_5.html
- **Who assigns.** The attorney owns the numeral key; the illustrator applies it and "the application team remains responsible for their meaning and final review". [D] https://annotra.com/how-to-add-reference-numbers-to-patent-drawings-a-complete-guide-for-illustrators/ Maas accepts an attorney-supplied list but does not require one. [C] https://maas-patent.de/patentzeichnung/
- **Common mistakes.** Skipping numbers without explanation, different numerals for one part, reuse for different parts, changing numbering mid-draft, not updating after revisions. [D] https://thepatentdrawing.com/reference-numerals-in-patents/

## Gaps and cautions

- No UK illustrator publishes a per-sheet or per-figure rate; the UK column is a gap. The German hourly and per-sheet pages do not show amounts beyond "ab EUR 35/Std".
- No illustrator firm publishes a pre-delivery QA checklist; section 5 is reconstructed from the rules and the USPTO examiner deck.
- No office publishes drawing-objection frequencies; the "37%" and "margins are #1" claims are vendor marketing.
- The AIPLA 2023 survey figure ($150-500 per sheet) is reported second-hand by a vendor and was not verified against the survey.
- The avvo.com and lawyerinfo.ca pages return HTTP 403 to automated fetches; their quotes come from search snippets of those pages.
